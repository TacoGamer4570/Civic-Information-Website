ballot/decoded is an independent, nonpartisan project. It isn't affiliated with any candidate, party, or campaign, and it doesn't tell you how to vote. It shows you who's closest to your answers.

Your answers never leave your browser. No accounts, no tracking.

how this works (the honest version)

1. Your scores

Each of the 22 questions is tied to one axis. Agreeing or disagreeing pushes you one way or the other. Questions you star count double, and skipped questions don't count at all.

  • Economy: −10 (most left) to +10 (most right). Taxes, wages, unions, regulation, spending.
  • Social: +10 (most liberal) to −10 (most conservative). Abortion, guns, policing, immigration, religion, voting.

2. Candidate positions

We place every candidate on the same two scales using their voting records, actions in office, campaign platforms, and news coverage. Where we know how they'd answer a specific quiz question, we record that too. Every placement shows a confidence level:

  • High: long public record on these issues.
  • Medium: some record, plus party and campaign positioning.
  • Low: new candidate or thin record. Treat it as a rough guess.

These are editorial estimates, not facts. Reasonable people would put some candidates a point or two somewhere else.

3. The match

Your match % starts with how close you are to a candidate on the two-axis map. When we know a candidate's stance on specific questions you answered, those count too (up to 45% of the score), so a candidate who agrees with you on the exact issues you care about gets a boost.

For ballot measures and judicial retention votes, we compare where a yes vote and a no vote land, plus your answer to any directly related question.

4. What this can't do

  • It's 2026, and most 2027 fields aren't final. People will enter, drop out, and lose primaries. We label everyone as declared, expected, exploring, or undecided.
  • Policy isn't everything. Experience, ethics, competence, and electability matter too, and a quiz can't measure them.
  • Judicial and retention races are about judging, not policy. We show which way your views lean, but judges aren't supposed to campaign on issues.
  • Statewide only. Your real ballot will also have local and legislative races.
